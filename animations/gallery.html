01

Twist matters. Area stays the same.

Presentation · p. 7

Equal area does not mean equal correction. The gold ring scans one spatial period of a stationary tube. The right panel shows the corresponding ellipse and its cell-gradient field. Rotating an ellipse gives positive energy; rotating a disc gives zero.

Open MP4Explore in 3D

Talk cue: “A disc does not notice rotation. An ellipse does.” The scan parameter is position, not physical time. The arrows are a corrector field, not particle velocities.

02

Largest deformation. Zero local energy.

Presentation · p. 8

The energy measures change along the channel. A section grows three lobes while preserving area and centroid. The film pauses at the middle station: the shape is most deformed, yet its first derivative along the axis vanishes, so the local energy is zero.

Open MP4Explore in 3D

Talk cue: “Distance from a disc is not the quantity we are measuring.” The average energy over the period is still positive. The tube remains fixed throughout the spatial scan.

03

A complicated section. A simple disc.

Presentation · p. 9

Move the calculation to a domain we understand. Matching coloured markers follow the polynomial map. A polar grid becomes a curved grid while crossing angles are preserved. On the reference disc, orthogonal monomials turn the energy into a finite sum.

Open MP4Explore in 3D

Talk cue: “We do not need to invert the map.” The displayed range, 0 ≤ m ≤ 0.22, stays below the cusp threshold m = 1/4.

04

A tangent predicts. Bounds control.

Presentation · p. 12

A first-order approximation specifies the initial slope. As ε increases, the tangent separates from the region allowed by two rigorous analytic bounds. The white dots are the paper’s eight cached, extrapolated three-dimensional FEM values.

Talk cue: “The exact coefficient is inside the band; the dots are numerical evidence.” No exact solution curve or certified numerical error bars are implied.
